The Burger King before Burger King

The story begins in 1952, when Mattoon residents Gene and Betty Hoots purchased the Frigid Queen ice cream shop.

Burgers, fries and other hot foods were added soon after, and the couple eventually gave the burger business a name of its own.

Gene had another name in mind, but Betty had the better idea: if there was a queen, there ought to be a king. Burger King was born.

The Hootses registered the Burger King name as an Illinois trademark in 1959. When the much larger Florida-based Burger King chain later expanded into Illinois, the dispute eventually reached federal court.

The 1968 decision protected the Mattoon restaurant's prior use of the name within roughly a 20-mile radius of Mattoon.

The case became a notable piece of U.S. trademark history — and helped make this hometown restaurant one of Mattoon's most unusual landmarks.
