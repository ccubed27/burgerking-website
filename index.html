More Than a Meal

The Burger King before Burger King

The story begins in 1952, when Gene and Betty Hoots purchased the Frigid Queen ice cream shop in Mattoon, Illinois.

Hamburgers, French fries and other hot foods were added soon after, transforming the small ice cream shop into a hometown restaurant.

When it came time to name the burger business, Betty Hoots had a simple idea: if there was a queen, there ought to be a king. Burger King was born.

The Hootses registered the Burger King name as an Illinois trademark in 1959. When the much larger Florida-based Burger King restaurant chain later expanded into Illinois, the dispute eventually reached federal court.

In 1968, a federal court decision protected the Mattoon restaurant's prior right to the name within roughly a 20-mile radius of Mattoon.

The case became a notable piece of American trademark history and helped turn this small hometown restaurant into one of Central Illinois' most unusual landmarks.

A True Local Original

More than seven decades of Mattoon history.

Long before national restaurant chains filled highways across America, this independent Burger King was serving Central Illinois.

Today, the name, the food and the story remain part of the community — a piece of local history you can still visit, eat and enjoy.
